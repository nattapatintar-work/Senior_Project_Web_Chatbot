### Nutrition source chosen and verified

**Online Thai Food Composition Database (INMU, Mahidol)** —
<https://inmu.mahidol.ac.th/thaifcd/>. Free for non-commercial use provided INMU is
acknowledged. Full citation and terms in `data/NUTRITION_SOURCE.md`.

It publishes each food only as a generated PDF — no JSON API, no bulk download — so
`tools/thaifcd.py` fetches and parses them, and `tools/build_cache.py` runs every lookup
once into a **committed** `data/thaifcd_cache.json`. Consequence: every nutrient value in
the eventual `recipes.json` is reproducible by re-running a script and reviewable in a git
diff, rather than being a number someone typed.

stored as `null`, never as 0 — a zero would be a fabricated measurement that looks

## 4. Issues Found and Fixed

**1. A parser that reported energy as carbohydrate.**
Searching the PDF text for the bare tag `CHOAVLDF` also matched its appearance *inside the
energy row*, which prints the formula `(4 x g protein) + (9 x g fat) + (4 x g CHOAVLDF)`.
The first hit was therefore the wrong row. Fixed by anchoring on printed label **plus**
tag. Worth recording because the failure produced a plausible number rather than an error
— glutinous rice would have been logged as 354 g of carbohydrate and nothing would have
complained.

**2. Composed dishes use different nutrient tags.**
Raw ingredients carry `ENERC` and `CHOAVLDF`. Group T dishes mostly leave those blank and
carry `ENERCT` and `CHOCDF`, and leave the derivation column as a dash. The first parser
returned all-`null` for every cooked dish. This is documented behaviour, not a data fault
— the database's own `ENERCT` row reads *"If CHOAVLDF was not available, CHOCDF was
used."* Fixed with fallbacks that **record which tag supplied each number**, so the
substitution is never invisible.

**3. The server drops connections under load.**
Two `ConnectionReset` and one read timeout during a single enumeration run. Added
retry-with-backoff (3s/6s/12s) that discards the session cookie on each retry — retrying
on a dead session just fails again more slowly — and raised the inter-request delay to
1.5s. This is an academic host, not a paid API.

**4. A search that reported absence where there was none.**
Searching group T for `ไข่เจียว` returned nothing, which looked like the database had no
Thai omelette. It has one — filed under **eggs**, as `ไข่ไก่, เจียว (เติมน้ำปลา)` (`H4`,
361 kcal/100 g), not under mixed foods. Lesson worth keeping: *a dish missing from group T
is not necessarily missing from the database.* Nearly caused several dishes to be
needlessly hand-computed.

## 5. The finding that changed the plan

**The national food table and our YOLO class list point in different directions.**

Thai FCD's composed-dish coverage is rich where the project is not, and empty where the
project needs it:

| Searched | Composed dishes found |
|---|---|
| แกง (curries) | ~35 |
| ข้าว (rice dishes) | ~28 |
| ผัดกะหล่ำปลี, ผัดคะน้า, ไก่ผัดขิง, ผัดฟักทอง | **0** |
| ต้มจืด, ไก่ย่าง, ยำวุ้นเส้น, สปาเกตตี | **0** |

Counting dishes whose main ingredients fall entirely inside the detectable 20 gives
**about 13 — and 11 of those are chicken curries.** Lettuce, cucumber, spinach, broccoli,
cauliflower, carrot, sweet potato, and green onion appear in essentially no composed dish.

The original intent — source all 40 directly from group T — is therefore incompatible with
the requirement that at least 20 recipes be reachable from a photo alone. Sourcing all 40
directly would also make a photo of chicken match eleven near-identical curries, which is a
weak demo and a weaker Week 7 evaluation.

**Resolution: two derivation methods, both sourced, each labelled per recipe.**

| Method | When | What is sourced | What is assumed |
|---|---|---|---|
| `direct` | dish exists in Thai FCD | everything | nothing |
| `computed` | it does not | per-ingredient nutrient values | **the gram amounts, chosen by us** |

Computed recipes record their gram amounts in a `nutrition_basis` field so a reader can
disagree with them specifically, and `tests/test_recipes.py` enforces that every computed
recipe actually shows its working. Cooking losses and oil absorption are **not** modelled.

> This mismatch is worth a paragraph in the Weeks 11–12 report. It is a real finding about
> building Thai food systems — the national reference table is organised around what
> Thais eat, while the detection classes were chosen around what a camera can see — not
> merely an inconvenience.
